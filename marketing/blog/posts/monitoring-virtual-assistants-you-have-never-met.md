You hired someone in Manila or Lagos or Karachi. You have seen their face once, on a video call that froze twice. They invoice you for 40 hours a week and you pay it, because what else are you going to do. If you want to monitor virtual assistants properly, the goal is not catching a liar. It is having a record that makes the invoice boring to approve.

I run BiBoTracking, a small time tracking tool, and most of the people who email me are in exactly this spot. They are not trying to police anyone. They just hate the feeling of wiring money against a number they cannot check.

## The invoice problem is a paperwork problem

Here is what usually happens without tracking. The VA sends a message at the end of the month: "40 hours/week, $600." Maybe there is a Google Sheet with dates and hours, filled in on Friday from memory. You approve it. You feel vaguely uneasy. You do it again next month.

Nothing in that flow is dishonest. But three things are broken:

- **You cannot reconstruct a day.** If a client asks what you spent on their project in week three, you have no answer.
- **You cannot have a conversation about pace.** You suspect the product descriptions are taking too long, but "too long" compared to what?
- **Disputes are unwinnable.** If you ever disagree about hours, it is your memory against theirs. Memory loses.

A tracker fixes the paperwork. It does not fix a bad hire, and it does not tell you whether the work was good. Keep those separate in your head or you will end up staring at screenshots trying to judge quality, which is a terrible way to judge quality.

## What to actually require before you pay

Be specific up front, in the contract or the first message. Vague monitoring is where resentment grows.

### Hours recorded by the app, not typed in

The whole point is that the record comes from the machine. A VA who types "8 hours" into a form has given you the same unverifiable number as before, just inside nicer software. Automatic capture means the clock starts when they work and stops when the laptop goes idle. I wrote about the difference between the two approaches in [automatic time tracking vs starting a timer by hand](/blog/automatic-time-tracking-vs-starting-a-timer-by-hand/) — for VAs, automatic is the only version that solves your problem.

### A screenshot interval you state out loud

Pick one and write it down. Every 10 minutes is the common default. Every 5 is unnecessary for most VA work and makes people feel hunted. Every 20 is fine if the work is long-form, like writing or video editing.

What matters more than the number is that they know it. "The app takes a screenshot roughly every 10 minutes while the timer runs, I look at them when something is unclear, I do not watch them live" is a sentence you can send in chat. It takes thirty seconds and removes most of the anxiety.

### An activity signal you understand the limits of

Keyboard and mouse activity tells you the machine was in use. That is it. A VA reading a 40-page brief will show near-zero activity for an hour and be doing exactly their job. A VA with a video playing in a background tab can show activity all afternoon.

So treat low activity as a question, never a verdict. "I saw a quiet stretch Tuesday afternoon, what were you on?" is a normal question. Docking pay over an activity percentage is how you lose a good VA.

### A weekly rhythm instead of daily watching

Once a week, fifteen minutes. Open the week, look at total hours per person, look at which projects absorbed the time, open screenshots only for the one block that looks odd. Then close it. If you are in the dashboard every day, you have bought yourself a new job.

## Monitor virtual assistants without turning it into surveillance

The hard part is not technical. It is that the person on the other end has probably been burned. A lot of VA work runs through platforms where monitoring is aggressive, scores are public, and a bad week costs them contracts. When you install a tracker, that is the frame they will assume.

Say three things on day one:

1. **What gets captured.** Work hours, periodic screenshots of the work screen, which apps and sites were used during tracked time. Nothing from their camera, nothing when the timer is off.
2. **When you look.** Weekly, plus when a client questions an invoice. Not in real time.
3. **What it is for.** Approving invoices and billing clients accurately. Not performance scoring.

Then follow your own rules. The first time you message someone at 2pm about a screenshot from 1:50pm, you have told them you are watching live, and no policy document will undo that.

Two more things that matter specifically for overseas VAs:

**The laptop is often theirs.** This is the big difference from employees. A VA on a personal machine is right to care where the tracker runs. Make it clear the tracker only records while the timer is running, and that they control the timer. If they do personal admin at lunch, the timer should be off, and there should be no penalty for that.

**Timezones make your "day" meaningless.** A VA eight hours ahead working their evening will look like they worked at 3am your time. Set the tracker and your reports to their timezone, not yours. Otherwise half your reviews start with you confused about a date boundary.

## Where a tracker is the wrong tool

I would rather you not buy anything than buy the wrong thing.

**You pay by deliverable.** If you pay $8 per product description or $300 per website, hours are irrelevant. Tracking them adds friction and an implied accusation for zero benefit. Pay for output and skip this entirely.

**You have one VA you trust and a flat monthly rate.** A fixed $500/month with no client billing behind it does not need an audit trail. You already decided the price is worth the work.

**The problem is the work, not the hours.** If the real issue is that the output is late, wrong, or thin, a tracker will not help. It will show you a person who was present at their desk while producing work you do not like. That is a scoping, feedback, or hiring conversation. I have watched people install monitoring hoping it would answer a performance question, and it just gave them more things to be unhappy about.

**You will not look at it.** Monitoring that nobody reviews is pure cost: the VA pays in discomfort, you pay in subscription. If you know you will never open the dashboard, do not install the app.
